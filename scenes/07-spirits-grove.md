---
title: The Spirit's Grove
---

Having completed the trials in the Halbrook spring cave, the party must complete the final challenge before an audience with the Woodland Spirit.

This scene serves to do the following things:

- Presents the party with the key decision, leading to the outcome of the adventure.
- Determines which of the "bosses" the party will face.

At the end of the scene, the party should hopefully:

- Have learned the truth behind the escaping horses.
- Have met the [Woodland Spirit](../characters/woodland-spirit.md).
- Either agreed to resolve the disharmony in Trawidok for the Woodland Spirit, _or_ elected to slay the Spirit and retrieve the missing horses.

The party can also obtain the following information:

- The reason why the "horses" find the villagers during the rite of passage.

## Setting

This scene takes place in: The Spirit's Grove.

The Spirit's Grove is a small, isolated sliver of the Feywild that has the appearance of a scenic meadow.
It is verdant, with saturated and vibrant colours, lush fields of healthy grass, hedges with wildflowers, and tranquil ponds scattered throughout.
The largest of these ponds is surrounded by reeds and acts as the "return" portal to the entrance of Halbrook spring cave.

When the party enters, they will be faced with [the final trial](#the-final-trial) and the corresponding "arena", before they enter into the meadow proper.

## The Final Trial

When the party arrives from the Halbrook spring cave, they will find themselves in the final trial arena.

This area is effectively walled by thick hedgerows, that upon investigation seem to go on forever and ascend into the sky.
Creatures cannot move through, over, or under, these hedgerows - the party must complete the puzzle to proceed.
Just in front of the party will be chessboard formed into the ground, using grass and mud squares to in place of the usual white and black grid pattern.
The hedges are tight to the edges of the chessboard, and on the far side are a number of large birds equal to the number of party members, as well as [Trohet](../characters/trohet.md), provided he is not otherwise occupied / dead.
For reference, the rank (line of 8 horizontal squares) of the board closest to the party will be referred to as the 1st rank, and the rank closest to Trohet the 8th rank.
The column (line of 8 vertical squares) of the board to the left of the party as they face Trohet is column `A`, and the rightmost column is column `H`, with the intermediate columns adopting the labels `B` through `G` from left to right.
This means that squares on the board can now be referred to by their algebraic notation, for example square `A4` refers to the square at the intersection of the 4h rank and leftmost (`A`) column.

On the party's side will be a number of spiritual horses - similar to those [encountered at the shrine](./05-into-the-woods.md) - waiting for the party to mount them.
At each of the horses' feet is a set of ceremonial armour that bears resemblance to that seen on the statue in the centre of Trawidok.

Upon entering the arena, the party can make checks to discern some information about their surroundings.

- (Nature DC 13 / Survival DC 15 / Arcana DC 16) To infer that the portal has transported the party an incredible distance, and they are very far from the cave they were just in.
  - Nature 15+ / Survival 17+: To identify that they are now in the Feywild, and that the portal has transferred them across planes.
- (Investigation DC 13 / AUTO: Proficiency in a suitable gaming set) To note that the pattern drawn in the grass ahead resembles a chess board.
  - Investigation 15+: There is a tangible aura of magic emanating from the board.
  - Arcana DC 15 / AUTO: Detect magic: To get the above information in a separate check.
  - Nature DC 13+: There are hoof-prints in the ground near the spectral horses. These are arranged to illustrate the valid ways in which a knight can move in chess (two "hoof-steps" horizontally then one vertically, or the reverse).
- (Nature DC 10 / Survival DC 12 / Animal Handling DC 9) Examining the birds on the other side of the grid gives the impression that they are "giant" versions of a regular fowl.
  - Nature 12+ / Animal Handling 11+: The birds resemble crows.
  - Nature 14+ / Animal Handling 12+: The creatures appear to be docile, and don't respond to stimuli like shouting or having feed thrown near them. As if they are waiting for something.
  - Nature 17+ / Animal Handling 14+: The birds look like crows, but they are more akin to rooks.
- (Insight DC 15 / Animal Handling DC 13) Reading Trohet's body language gives the impression that he is beckoning the party to join him.
- (History DC 13 / ADV: The party talked to Hierim about Trawidok's military history or read the statue plaque in the village square) The armour laid at the feet of the spectral horses is very similar to that which appears on the statue in Trawidok town square.
  - 14+: It is almost identical in fact, but is missing the wings.

To progress, the party needs to don the armour next to the horses and mount one each, before stepping onto the chessboard's first rank.
The spectral horses will refuse to step further than the first rank, will not occupy the same square as another horse, and will refuse to move once on a valid square until the trial starts.
As each party member and their horse enters the grid, a rook will enter the same column on the 8th rank, getting into position for the start of the trial.
Once all party members are mounted and have taken up position on the board, the trial begins.

To run the trial, have the party roll initiative to determine their move order, which will be conducted as per rounds of combat.
Take note of the rook that is opposite each player at the start of the trial - a rook moves immediately after its corresponding player.
On each turn, a party member can move their horse as a knight moves in chess - either 2 squares horizontally then 1 vertically, or 2 squares vertically then 1 square horizontally.
Knights may move through other pieces (hostile or friendly) but may not end their turn on a square occupied by a friendly piece.
Ending a move on a square occupied by a rook ("capturing the rook" as per chess lingo) removes the rook from the board - after 1d4+2 turns, that rook re-enters the board on the 8th rank, in the same column as the party member that captured it.
Attempting to make an illegal move as a knight results in that knight being treated as having been captured by a rook (see below).

The objective of the party members is to either:

- Have all party members and their horses move "off" the board, as if moving to the non-existant 9th or 10th rank.
  This completes the trial for that party member (they can dismount and doff the armour) and causes their corresponding rook to disappear permanently from the board (if it is currently on the board), and that rook will never re-enter the board.
- Manage to have no rooks on the board at the _start_ of a round of combat - note that previously-captured rooks may re-enter the board before the "round" ends to prevent this.
  Once this is achieved, the trial is completed for all party members, and they can freely move across the board to join Trohet.

Deliberately moving through ("jumping over") another piece on the board, or capturing a rook, causes the horse that did so to grow wings and reveal that it is actually a pegasus.

The rooks move as, well, rooks do in chess - they may move any number of squares horizontally or vertically on a turn.
They may not move through other pieces, cannot end their turn on a square occupied by another rook, but can "capture" the knights by finishing their move on a square occupied by a knight.
Rooks will _not_ capture a knight that has not yet taken a turn on the first round of combat, after this, it is fair game.
When a rook captures a knight, it's rider takes 1d4+1 force damage and is ejected from the board, back towards the portal.
The rider may return to the board after 1d4 turns, on which turn they can take up position on the 1st rank of the board again (but may not move any further until the next turn).

After completing the trial, Trohet will lead the party into the meadow.
He will also reveal his own set of wings, making him a pegasus.
The party may chose to ask him questions if they have a means of communicating with him, but he will just defer their answers to the Spirit in the Grove.

## Meet the Spirit

Upon being lead by Trohet into the meadow, the party will spot the missing Trawidok horses - or rather pegasid - grazing, running, and sleeping in the meadow.
Standing in the centre of the meadow, and approaching the party when they come into vision, will be the Woodland Spirit.
The other pegasid will also turn away from their other activities to listen to the conversation between the Spirit and the party, when it starts.

- (Perception DC 15, PASSIVE) The water of the largest pond is shimmering, in a similar way that the portal from the spring cave did.

If the Spirit's trials were completed, it will be pleased and introduce itself as the guardian of Trawidok forest, and explain its role in maintaining balance between nature and people.
It will also give the full story behind the legend of Trawidok and explain the reason behind the offer that was made, and how this evolved into the Rite of Passage the village uses today.
The pegasid voluntarily give up their wings to learn to live in harmony with the village, bonding with a villager that undergoes the Rite and appears to share similar ideals, and returning to the forest upon their death.
As can be imaged, the Spirit views the Baron's actions as a threat to the balance of nature, and an affront to his kin.
It will also be somewhat chastising towards the younger generation of Trawidok who sold their parents' horses to the Baron, but will be more reasonable if told that the Baron was being coercive.

Regardless, after the information exchange, the Spirit will present the party with the task of ensuring balance is restored to the forest.
This can be achieved by either freeing all the Trawidok-native horses currently being held against their will in the Baron's racecourse (by any means), _or_ driving the Baron and his cronies out of Trawidok.
The racecourse can be preserved if the management changes and the villagers return to using horses from the Rite that _want_ to partake in races.

If the party accepts the task, the Spirit will thank them and invite them to retrieve [the Feywild Horsewhistle](../items/feywild-horsewhistle.md) from the reeds near the portal-pond.
They will also explain that the pond can be used as a portal to return to the spring cave entrance.
If Hierim or Zanke are in danger from the Baron (due to earlier party actions), the Spirit will also give a further warning that it now senses deep imbalance within the village.

The party may reject the task, in which case the Spirit will simply invite them to leave.
The Horsewhistle cannot be retrieved in this eventuality, though the party can still restore balance to the village inadvertently through their future actions against the Baron.
They will not be rewarded for doing so, in this event, however.

The party can also express their objective is - or simply attempt - to apprehend the missing pegasid for the Baron.
This triggers the combat encounter listed below, and turns the Spirit hostile.
Balance cannot be restored to the forest in this event, and returning the horses to the Baron ensures that he remains in charge and Trawidok continues to decline.
Any village NPCs at risk to the Baron also fall foul of his machinations.

## Combat Encounter - Fighting the Spirit

Fighting the Woodland Spirit turns it hostile, along with all the other pegasid in the meadow (including Trohet).
However, killing the Woodland Spirit will turn all the other pegasid docile, and they will loose their wings (reverting to regular horses).

### Encounter Information

Trigger: The party displays hostile actions or intentions towards the occupants of the meadow.
Intended difficulty: Hard

Enemies:

- [The Woodland Spirit](FIXME)
- [Trohet](../characters/trohet.md)
- As many pegasid as have escaped from Trawidok thus far.
  They use [Trohet's stat block](../characters/trohet.md#statblock).

Enemy Objectives:

- The Woodland Spirit and pegasid will attempt to kill the party.

### Initial Locations

As determined during the conversation.
The pegasid should be in a loose circle around the party, if the conversation went on for a modest amount of time.

### Other Encounter Factors

Killing the Woodland Spirit will cause all (remaining alive) pegasid to become docile.
They will loose their wings and revert to regular horses, and can be corralled by the party through the portal and back to Trawidok in this state.

### Loot

None.
Pegasid that are killed dissipate like the spectral horses the party has encountered before, leaving no remains.

## Next Scenes

However the party resolves the encounter in the meadow, they can exit via the pond-portal to the entrance of Halbrook spring cave.
They will return to the village to [confront the Baron](./08-confrontations.md) and complete the adventure.
